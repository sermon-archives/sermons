## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully expounds Romans 8:12-13 by grounding the fight against sin in the indicative reality of the Spirit’s indwelling, not mere command. It correctly distinguishes believers from unbelievers, showing that the battle is uniquely Christian and Spirit-empowered. The pastoral warning that ongoing, unopposed sin calls one’s salvation into question aligns with Scripture’s teaching on perseverance and the necessity of sanctification as evidence of regeneration.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon overemphasizes the fight as the defining mark of a Christian, risking a performance-based assurance that neglects the finished work of Christ. It does not explicitly ground the Spirit’s power in Christ’s substitutionary death and resurrection, nor does it balance the call to mortify sin with the comfort of the gospel for those who struggle. The application that every command is a fight could foster legalism rather than rest in grace.
