## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully exegetes Matthew 26:47-56, highlighting Jesus' sovereign control even in betrayal. It rightly affirms Christ's voluntary, substitutionary death (v. 56: 'that the Scriptures might be fulfilled') and counters any notion of victimhood. The pastoral application—that believers can trust Christ's plan despite present suffering—is grounded in the text. The gospel is present: Jesus died to save sinners, and the call to trust Him is clear.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon omits Jesus’ bodily resurrection and return, essential to the gospel hope. Sanctification is mentioned only as 'still sinning' and 'running back to the cross,' but lacks a clear role for the Spirit’s ongoing work. The statement that Jesus died for 'any sins we will commit' can imply license or diminish the need for active repentance. Application focuses on doubt about God’s plan but neglects the text’s warning about forsaking Christ (v. 56).
