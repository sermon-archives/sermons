## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully exposits Exodus 25:1-22, showing God’s command for a voluntary offering from a willing heart. It connects the passage to New Testament giving (2 Corinthians 9:7) and emphasizes that preaching God’s Word is central to worship and disciple-making. The doctrine of substitutionary atonement is clear through the typology of the mercy seat as propitiation (Romans 3:25), grounding salvation in Christ’s blood satisfying God’s wrath. The pastoral application urges hearers to examine whether God dwells among them or in them, calling for repentance and eager service.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon’s treatment of human sin and judgment is underdeveloped; while it mentions God’s wrath against violators of the law, it does not clearly articulate universal sinfulness or the necessity of personal repentance for salvation. The gospel presentation assumes hearers understand 'substitutionary atonement' without explicitly connecting faith in Christ’s finished work to justification. Applications for sanctification focus heavily on giving and ministry involvement, but the text’s emphasis on God’s dwelling presence as the basis for holy living is not fully explored, risking a pragmatic view of discipleship.
