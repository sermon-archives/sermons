## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully expounds Matthew 26:36-44, centering on Jesus' command to 'watch and pray' against temptation. It rightly highlights Christ's full humanity in His sorrow and submission, modeling prayer as aligning one's will with the Father's rather than bending God to ours. The call to pray before acting, and the warning against self-reliance from the sleeping disciples, are practical and biblically grounded. The gospel is implicit in Christ's solitary journey to the cross, and the need for Spirit-enabled dependence is clear.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon nearly omits the substitutionary atonement—Jesus' prayer concerning 'the cup' is mentioned but not explained as God's wrath for sin, which weakens gospel clarity. There is no explicit call to repentance or faith in Christ's finished work; the application leans heavily on human effort in prayer without linking it to grace or the Spirit's enabling for sanctification. The opening story, while illustrative, risks moralizing (listen to father's instructions) rather than grounding application directly in the text's redemptive context.
