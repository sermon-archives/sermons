## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully anchors its central exhortation to unity in the immediate context of 1 Peter 3:8, drawing out the five qualities listed. It rightly distinguishes biblical unity from cultic coercion, warning against abusive leadership that demands unquestioning submission. The preacher correctly upholds the Berean model (Acts 17:11) of testing teaching by Scripture. The connection between unity of mind and humility of mind is exegetically sound, mirroring Philippians 2:2-5, and the pastoral application towards self-sacrifice for the gospel’s advance is clear and concrete.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon focuses almost exclusively on the first and last qualities (unity and humility) while giving little doctrinal weight to Christ's specific person and work. The substitutionary death is mentioned only as a truth claim ('Christ died on the cross'), not as the ground of unity or a basis for grace. It lacks a clear gospel explanation of sin, repentance, justification, or adoption. The view of sanctification is primarily behavioral (getting along) rather than Spirit-empowered transformation rooted in the finished work of Christ. Application risks moralism, urging unity without thoroughly grounding it in the indicatives of the gospel.
