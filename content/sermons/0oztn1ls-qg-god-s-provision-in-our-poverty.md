## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully grounds Romans 8:26-27 in its redemptive-historical context, tracing the letter's theme of God's righteousness from condemnation to justification to sanctification. It rightly identifies the Holy Spirit's intercessory role as the key provision for believers' weakness, upholding Christ's mediating work. The application wisely connects the Spirit's prayers to God's will for conformity to Christ (Romans 8:29), avoiding moralism by centering on divine grace rather than human effort. The pastoral emphasis on suffering between justification and glorification is biblically sound.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon presents sanctification primarily as 'weaning ourselves from sin' (Romans 6-8), which risks implying human cooperation rather than Spirit-empowered mortification. While noting the Spirit's role in victory, it underemphasizes the believer's active dependence on the Spirit through faith (Galatians 5:16). The gospel call at the end is brief and lacks explicit mention of repentance from sin and faith in Christ's substitutionary death and bodily resurrection. The sermon could more clearly connect the Spirit's intercession to the finished work of Christ (Hebrews 7:25), as the passage's assurance rests on Christ's completed redemption.
