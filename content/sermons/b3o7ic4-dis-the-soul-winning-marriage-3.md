## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully expounds 1 Peter 3:7 within its immediate context, correctly connecting 'likewise' to the preceding chain of submission passages (1 Pet 2:13–3:6). It grounds its call for husbands in the pattern of Christ's self-sacrifice, emphasizing that Christian marriage displays the gospel to a watching world. The pastoral application is clear and direct, urging repentance and transformation by renewing the mind. The preacher explicitly distinguishes the commands from the gospel, warning that obedience does not save but flows from saving grace.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon lacks a clear presentation of human sin and judgment as the universal problem requiring Christ's substitutionary death. While it mentions grace, it does not articulate justification or adoption as the basis for Christian living. The call to 'repent' and 'be transformed' is present, but the role of the Holy Spirit in sanctification is entirely omitted. The exposition risks reducing marriage instruction to a set of pragmatic rules for 'soul-winning' without adequately grounding it in the indicative of who believers are in Christ.
