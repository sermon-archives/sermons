## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully expounds Matthew 26:36-56, highlighting Jesus' genuine human sorrow and his unique substitutionary suffering under divine wrath. It correctly affirms Christ's sinlessness, deity (legions of angels), and the atonement's cost. The pastoral application—that believers will never experience that wrath—rightly motivates gratitude and endurance. The connection between Gethsemane and the cross is exegetically sound, and the call to take up one's cross is rooted in Christ's example.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The gospel is implicit but lacks clarity on repentance and faith as the sole means of receiving salvation. Sanctification is mentioned vaguely ('take up your cross') without explicit reference to the Holy Spirit's enabling. The claim that Matthew was written as a 'manual for disciple makers' to 'doubting Christians' is an interpretive leap not directly supported by the text. The sermon also omits justification and adoption, focusing almost exclusively on the atonement's intensity.
