## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully exposits 1 Peter 3:3-6, emphasizing the priority of inner spiritual beauty over external adornment. It correctly distinguishes submission as voluntary and limited by obedience to God, avoiding a blanket endorsement of sinful commands. The call to follow Sarah’s example of hope in God is biblically grounded, and the closing mention of Christ’s death for sinners and the need for repentance and forgiveness points to the gospel. The pastoral concern to address fear with faith in God’s promises is a helpful application.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The gospel is introduced only as an afterthought, not integrated into the main exposition; the sermon risks implying that changed behavior (inner beauty) is the primary means of soul-winning rather than the proclamation of Christ’s substitutionary work. The heavy focus on cultural statistics and external appearance may overshadow the text’s call to hope in God. Sanctification is presented largely as human effort to follow Sarah’s example, with little emphasis on the Spirit’s enabling power to produce gentleness and quietness from a heart transformed by grace.
