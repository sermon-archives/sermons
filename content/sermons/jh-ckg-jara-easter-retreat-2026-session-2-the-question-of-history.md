## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully exegetes Matthew 26:39, centering on Jesus’ agony and prayer as evidence that the cross was necessary, not optional. It affirms Christ’s true humanity through his sorrow and weakness, and his submission to the Father’s will. The preacher correctly ties the necessity of the atonement to exclusive salvation by grace through faith, rejecting human effort or multiple ways to God. The application calls believers to willingness to suffer for Christ, consistent with New Testament teaching on disciple and sanctification.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The sermon fails to explicitly articulate Christ’s substitutionary death as bearing God’s wrath for sin and does not mention his bodily resurrection or return, which are essential to the gospel. The connection between Jesus’ prayer and the necessity of the cross is asserted but lacks engagement with Old Testament or theological context (e.g., covenantal promises, priesthood). The application on suffering lacks nuance, potentially implying that all Christian suffering directly advances the gospel, which may overextend the text’s specific focus on Christ’s unique mission.
