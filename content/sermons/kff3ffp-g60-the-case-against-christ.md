## Strengths (biblical review)

The sermon faithfully draws the central contrast between Jesus’ steadfastness and Peter’s weakness, grounding applications in the text (Matthew 26:57-68). It rightly affirms Christ’s sinless submission to unjust suffering as both substitutionary atonement (He endured judgment “so he could die for sinners”) and an example for believers (1 Peter 2:21). The gospel is clearly presented: Christ is the saving, sovereign King who demands repentance, and salvation requires bowing to Him.

## Weaknesses / biblical cautions

The handling of the passage lacks exegetical depth on the specific charges and OT background; the trial narrative is summarized without careful interaction with the false witnesses or Caiaphas’s question. The doctrine of substitutionary atonement is asserted but not developed (e.g., why Christ’s silence and suffering satisfy divine wrath). Sanctification is reduced primarily to following Christ’s example of silence under accusation, which risks moralism and neglects the Spirit’s internal work. The application for unbelievers (simply “kiss the Son”) lacks clarity on faith and repentance as God’s gifts.
